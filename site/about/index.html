Our Mission

About This Site

Tender Root exists to be a gentle companion on your journey with Chinese medicine — because the ideas are genuinely interesting, and most coverage is either too mystical or too dry.

What we do

We explain the core concepts of Traditional Chinese Medicine — Yin and Yang, Qi, the Five Phases, the organ networks, the meridian system — in clear, plain language, always showing the original Chinese terms and their Pinyin. Our wellness articles translate classical self-care advice into habits a modern reader can actually try: seasonal eating, sleep timing, acupressure and everyday teas.

We are deliberately bilingual in spirit: every article keeps the Chinese term visible, because the terms are the door. Once you can read "Liver qi stagnation" as 肝气郁结 (gān qì yù jié), a whole literature opens up.

How we write

Respect for two audiences guides us. To readers curious about TCM, we promise no mysticism, no miracle claims, and honest language about what is classical theory versus what modern research explores. To the tradition itself, we promise fidelity: when we describe a concept, we describe it as its own literature does, not as a straw man.

Where science has something to say — acupuncture research, sleep architecture, herb–drug interactions — we say it plainly. Where TCM speaks in its own theoretical language, we translate rather than force-fit.

Who this is for

Curious readers who met TCM at an acupuncture clinic or a bubble-tea shop and wondered what the words mean. Students beginning formal study who want a plain-language companion. Practitioners looking for clear ways to explain concepts to patients. And anyone who simply enjoys a 2,000-year-old conversation about how bodies work.

Read in any order. Start with Yin and Yang if you are new; jump to the glossary whenever a term loses you.
