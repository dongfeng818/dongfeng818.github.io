TCM Glossary

Key terms of Traditional Chinese Medicine with Chinese characters and Pinyin. These words appear throughout the site — use this page as your reference.

Term Chinese Pinyin What it means
Qi 气 qì The animating, functional energy of the body — digestion, warmth, movement, immunity.
Yin / Yang 阴阳 yīn yáng The two complementary aspects of all things; health is their dynamic balance.
Five Phases 五行 wǔ xíng Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water — a dynamic model linking organs, emotions, seasons and flavours.
Zang-fu organs 脏腑 zàng fǔ The five solid storage networks (zang) and six hollow transformation organs (fu).
Meridians 经络 jīng luò The channels through which qi and blood circulate, linking organs with the surface.
Acupuncture point 穴位 xué wèi A specific location on a meridian where the flow can be stimulated from the surface.
Acupressure 穴位按压 xué wèi àn yā Stimulation of acupuncture points with finger pressure instead of needles.
Moxibustion 艾灸 ài jiǔ Warming points or regions with burning dried mugwort (moxa).
Blood (TCM) 血 xuè The nourishing substance that moistens tissues and anchors the mind — broader than the Western term.
Body fluids 津液 jīn yè All the moistening liquids: saliva, sweat, tears, synovial fluid, mucous secretions.
Jing (essence) 精 jīng The deep inherited reserve stored in the Kidneys, governing growth, reproduction and ageing.
Shen (spirit) 神 shén The vitality of consciousness, housed by the Heart; visible as sparkle, sleep and presence.
Dampness 湿 shī A pathological heaviness/stickiness: foggy head, heavy limbs, bloating, greasy tongue coating.
Heat / Fire 热 / 火 rè / huǒ A pattern of excess activity: thirst, redness, agitation, inflammation-like signs.
Deficiency 虚 xū A pattern of insufficiency of a vital substance — fatigue, weakness, pallor.
Stagnation 瘀 / 滞 yū / zhì Impaired flow of qi or blood: distension, fixed pain, frustration, purple tongue.
Food therapy 食疗 shí liáo Using the thermal nature and flavour of everyday foods to gently correct imbalance.
Congee 粥 zhōu Slow-cooked rice porridge, the classic gentle food for weak digestion and recovery.
Gua sha 刮痧 guā shā Scraping the skin with a smooth tool to move surface qi and blood.
Cupping 拔罐 bá guàn Suction cups on the skin to draw circulation to an area and release surface tension.
